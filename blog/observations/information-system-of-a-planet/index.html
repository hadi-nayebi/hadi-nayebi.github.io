Observation 1

The Information System of a Planet

Long before books, cities, or machines, Earth was already storing information. At first, the archive was biological: patterns persisted because living things reproduced them. Then nervous systems let organisms learn within a lifetime. Much later, one species learned to move increasingly complex information between minds, preserve it outside the body, and build on what earlier minds had discovered.

This is the story of that second layer — how information escaped the genome, then the individual brain, and gradually became something a civilization could accumulate.

The story begins with life itself.

Before information could travel between minds, it first had to survive in bodies — and then become something an organism could learn.
