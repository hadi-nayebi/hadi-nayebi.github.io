The Information System of a Planet

A species learns. Then minds learn to share. Memory escapes the skull. Ideas survive their inventors. Across a very long history, the planet builds an information layer on top of biology.

Memory What can survive one mind, one body, or one generation?
Movement How far, how fast, and how faithfully can information travel?
Collective capability What becomes possible when many minds can accumulate and work on the same information?

Read this like a story, not a timeline.

This is a growing sequence of episodes. Each episode is its own slideshow: one image, then the complete text that image is meant to carry. Some slides move quickly. Others stay with a dense idea for several minutes. Sources sit underneath for readers who want to inspect the evidence or go deeper.

The point is not to claim that one invention mechanically caused the next. Cities can create pressure for records; records can make larger cities easier to coordinate. Trade can demand better communication; better communication can expand trade. The interesting thing is the feedback loop: information tools change what groups of humans can do, and those new capabilities create pressure for the next tool.

Audio is part of the format. Every slide reserves a narration slot from the day it is published. Hadi adds the recordings separately; when they are available, a reader can listen slide by slide or start an episode and let the audio advance it.

The observation has a frame. The first episode comes next.

Episodes will appear here after each one has been researched, illustrated, reviewed, and merged.
