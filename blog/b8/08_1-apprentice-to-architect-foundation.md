[Essay 7.9](../b7/07_9-creating-a-new-plugin.html) closed the plugin kit with a walkthrough — guiding your seed to author a brand-new plugin, part by part, under the lock ceremony's friction. That essay handed you the *capacity* to grow the brain. This essay opens what happens once that capacity is yours.

A seed is not a standard product that gets installed once and then used unchanged. It is a distinct system a user and agent assemble, then *grow*, cycle by cycle, under the operator's care. A new seed in week one looks different from the same seed in month three — not only because its memory has changed, but because its structures, plugins, and adaptations may have changed with the work. Shared design principles may remain recognizable; the software does not need to stay identical. *[ref: seed-grows-cycle-by-cycle | private historical prototype | Checked against a private historical prototype; identifying repository, revision, source paths, and unpublished implementation details are omitted.]*

The load-bearing claim of this series is this. The seed grows along several parallel axes — one earlier Claude-based reference architecture names three of them: jobs take increasingly structured forms; behavioral controls migrate inward from soft voice to hard hook to fossilized template; and the operator's relationship with the seed shifts from supervising its cognition to composing with it. Each axis has its own pace, its own evidence, its own gates. None is automatic. And — the part most operators do not expect — *the brain stops growing in size while never stopping learning*. The architecture is bounded by design; the substrate the brain rests on is not. *[ref: three-growth-axes-prototype-design | private historical prototype | Checked against a private historical prototype; identifying repository, revision, source paths, and unpublished implementation details are omitted.]*

That last claim is what makes the seed sustainable across long timescales. A brain that grew without bound would drown its own cognition. A brain that stopped learning would stop adapting. The seed does both: caps and compression push the brain toward a stable equilibrium, while the knowledge directory and the plugin kit absorb the durable patterns the brain compresses away. The seed does not try to preserve every transient detail in the working brain. It preserves what keeps mattering, and stores most of that somewhere other than the brain. *[ref: brain-bounded-knowledge-unbounded | private historical prototype | Checked against a private historical prototype; identifying repository, revision, source paths, and unpublished implementation details are omitted.]*

A patent attorney could read the parts that follow for the same reason an ML researcher could: the *pattern* of seed maturation transfers across every operator's domain. The patterns the attorney's seed codifies will not look like the researcher's, but the *shape* of how patterns get codified — voice fires, measurement accumulates, hook hardens, template inherits — is reusable. Nothing in this essay is automatic; every move to a more structured form requires evidence the operator and seed agree on. *[ref: soft-to-hard-migration-pipeline | private historical prototype | Checked against a private historical prototype; identifying repository, revision, source paths, and unpublished implementation details are omitted.]*
