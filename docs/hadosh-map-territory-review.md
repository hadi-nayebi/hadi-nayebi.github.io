# Hadosh Academy Through “The Map Is Not the Territory” — Review Record

## Purpose

Lens 2 tests a central risk in Hadosh Academy's own work: a clear public description of user-owned agency can be mistaken for the capability it describes. The article distinguishes the Academy's public map, the model's computational adaptation, and the user's operating territory.

## Public baseline

- Lens 1, **First Principles**, is the published series baseline.
- Start Here already establishes model–harness separation, ownership, continuity, authority, and local adaptation.
- Public Origin and dashboard writings establish that interfaces represent operational state but remain distinct from the work and the owner's acceptance.
- The Great Mental Models provides the series frame; the article applies the map–territory lens to current Hadosh architecture rather than summarizing a book chapter.

## Issues examined

1. Public principles could sound like a prescribed universal harness.
2. A dashboard or status could be treated as proof of capability.
3. An agent's summary of a user could harden into uncorrectable truth.
4. One giant assistant could erase meaningful boundaries between work, family, client, creative, and public worlds.
5. Implementation stories could be generalized without sufficient repeated evidence.

## Editorial decisions

- Center the thesis: **a sophisticated description of user-owned agency is not yet user-owned agency**.
- Define strong evidence as durable, inspectable, repeatedly useful capability retained by the user.
- Present Hadosh context as a guide for model computation, not an identical installation recipe.
- Make a portfolio of compartmentalized harness worlds an explicit consequence of the lens.
- Keep private territory private while allowing generalized, reviewed field evidence to improve public patterns.
- End with five questions readers can apply to an AI workflow.

## Evidence and uncertainty

- The article makes architectural and evaluative claims, not claims that one universal Hadosh implementation exists.
- Examples are illustrative and do not disclose private repositories or user work.
- The article does not claim that several dashboards are always better; it argues that compartmentalization can express real authority and privacy boundaries.
- Publication, narration, and derivative social content remain separate decisions.
