# Mental-model article production and backward-audit method

## Purpose

This is the production method for **Hadosh Through Mental Models**. It is a method for developing and revising the series, not a fixed article outline. Different lenses may need different stories, examples, lengths, and section structures.

The governing principle is bidirectional:

1. a new lens must interpret the complete accepted context rather than only the newest idea; and
2. the context developed by that lens must be tested against relevant earlier public surfaces.

Publication creates a usable, versioned synthesis. It does not freeze the corpus.

## The unit of work

A lens job owns more than the newest article. Its review unit contains:

- the new or revised canonical article and rendered page;
- the lens-specific visual and contribution surface;
- the accumulated thesis it must preserve or explicitly challenge;
- the semantically related earlier articles, diagrams, instructions, features, navigation, and metadata;
- an origin-to-current change record;
- an exact backward-audit ledger;
- open questions with a reason and reopening condition;
- verification evidence and the explicit human decision requested.

A job must not inspect the entire website indiscriminately. It identifies a **semantic neighborhood**: surfaces that make the same claim, define the same abstraction, teach the same behavior, or would mislead readers if left unchanged. The ledger records why each surface was included.

## Production cycle

### 1. Recover the accumulated context

Read the live branch, article history, prior review records, active job, and accepted Hadi decisions. Separate:

- stable foundations;
- current synthesis;
- illustrative examples;
- proposals;
- unresolved questions;
- superseded wording.

A recent comment does not override the accumulated thesis merely because it is recent.

### 2. Establish the shared model

Before polishing prose, state:

- what the mental model means in plain language;
- the exact question it asks of Hadosh;
- what it uniquely contributes beyond prior lenses;
- which earlier claims it may test;
- what evidence supports the interpretation;
- what remains unknown.

If this model is disputed, the article stays in Draft.

### 3. Design the reader journey

Choose a narrative suited to the lens. The method requires a beginning, tension, development, and payoff, but does not prescribe identical headings.

The article should normally give the reader:

- a clear explanation of the lens;
- a concrete scenario or operational example;
- a distinction that changes how the system is understood;
- a compact way to apply the lens elsewhere;
- visible uncertainty where it materially affects interpretation.

### 4. Run forward synthesis

Draft the lens from the complete context. Verify that it adds an angle rather than merely restating the Academy thesis. Label architectural claims, prospective outcomes, examples, and demonstrated evidence accurately.

### 5. Run the backward audit

Search the semantic neighborhood for every claim materially affected by the new context. For each surface, record one disposition:

- **revise** — exact wording or meaning should change now;
- **inspected—no change** — the existing surface already remains accurate;
- **open question** — the impact is plausible but evidence is insufficient;
- **new job** — the effect is real but belongs to a separately bounded implementation or publication unit.

A revision proposal must quote or summarize the prior claim, state the new context, provide the exact proposed sentence or paragraph, and explain why the change is proportional.

### 6. Route open questions

No question is closed merely to make the article or diagram appear complete.

Every open question records:

- why the current context is insufficient;
- whether it affects public interpretation or only internal operations;
- what evidence, implementation, comparison, or decision would reopen it;
- where it is retained.

The final public/internal routing rule is pending [Departments Issue #123](https://github.com/hadi-nayebi/hadosh_academy_departments/issues/123).

### 7. Apply editorial and adversarial passes

At minimum, inspect:

- conceptual fidelity to the lens;
- fidelity to accumulated Academy context;
- narrative clarity and progression;
- reader utility and transferability;
- evidence discipline and treatment of unknowns;
- human authority and public/private boundaries;
- backward-audit completeness;
- accessibility and visual contribution;
- canonical Markdown/HTML parity;
- navigation, metadata, version, and reading-time accuracy.

Scores are audit judgments, not proof. A high average cannot hide a material contradiction, unsupported claim, privacy breach, or missing backward audit.

### 8. Prepare the review packet

Hadi's review surface must contain:

1. the lens's origin and purpose;
2. the complete current article;
3. what changed since the prior review and why;
4. what remains stable;
5. the scorecard with evidence;
6. the backward-audit ledger, including inspected/no-change surfaces;
7. open questions divided into answerable now and intentionally deferred;
8. exact proposed wording for every earlier public surface;
9. verification state;
10. one exact disposition requested.

Mergeability is technical evidence, not conceptual approval.

### 9. Absorb the decision and verify

After Hadi's response:

- apply accepted changes to every affected canonical and rendered surface;
- update review records and version metadata;
- rerun repository validation on the exact head;
- return the PR to ready-for-review only when the decision issues are resolved;
- keep narration and social derivatives separate unless explicitly authorized.

### 10. Learn after publication

Later comments, implementations, failures, and field observations begin as proposals or evidence. They may:

- correct wording directly;
- reopen a question;
- require comparison across several cases;
- support a reusable pattern;
- expose a flaw in an earlier lens.

When accepted context changes, run this cycle again. The article history should show why the map changed.

## Backward-audit ledger format

| Field | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| Surface | Repository path and public role |
| Prior claim | Exact wording or faithful claim summary |
| New context | What the current lens or evidence adds |
| Disposition | revise / inspected—no change / open question / new job |
| Exact proposal | Full replacement or insertion when revision is proposed |
| Reason | Why the effect is material and proportional |
| Version effect | patch / minor / major working classification |
| Approval | pending / accepted / revised / declined, with decision link |

Working version convention:

- **patch** — wording, link, metadata, or example changes without a conceptual change;
- **minor** — accepted new context or a new section that extends the thesis without replacing its foundations;
- **major** — a material change to the article's governing thesis or foundations.

## Recency-bias check for new context

New input is classified before it changes the corpus:

1. Does it alter a foundation, extend an existing claim, clarify an example, or merely supply another implementation possibility?
2. Which existing claims support it, and which constraints prevent it from becoming the new center of gravity?
3. What is the smallest proportional revision that preserves the accumulated argument?
4. Which established sections and examples should remain unchanged?
5. Does the review record explain why the change is additive, corrective, or foundational?

The filmmaker refinement is classified as an **additive clarification**. It strengthens the existing claims about compartmentalization, selected cognitive externalization, retained correction, and human authority. It does not change the article sequence, the five first-principles foundations, the nested map–territory model, or the evidentiary and privacy boundaries.

## Questions intentionally left open

These should not be forced into answers at the current stage:

- the universal mechanism, if any, by which comments become stable Academy context;
- the evidence threshold at which a pattern has crystallized across meaningfully different harnesses;
- the eventual population-scale effect on independent work and employment;
- a universal ontology or interoperability scheme for distinct user-owned harnesses.

Each requires evidence from later discussions, implementations, or comparisons. Their open status is part of the current truth.
