Agent Systems · Harness Engineering

Externalizing Human Agency Into a Digital Cortex

I build and compare agent harnesses to understand how parts of a person's cognition can be mapped into a persistent, inspectable system around an LLM — a digital cortex that remembers, works, learns, and evolves with its user.

The engineering problem underneath that vision is abstraction: finding concepts that are precise enough for an agent to implement, but intuitive enough for a non-programmer to understand and use to shape their own harness.

How I Build It Technical Discussion
Core Thesis

The LLM Is the Engine. The Harness Is the Digital Cortex.

A CLI agent gives an LLM tools and access to a local environment. The model supplies general reasoning capability; the harness supplies the persistent structure around it — memory, behavioral controls, jobs, cognitive modes, state, permissions, interfaces, and learned patterns.

My goal is not to make a generic artificial worker that belongs to a platform. It is to help a person externalize some of their own agency into a system they own. An idea can leave the biological cortex, enter the digital cortex as a job or new behavior, continue developing while the person is doing something else, and return later for the judgment only the person should make.

That is why ownership and legibility matter. A mature harness becomes intimate infrastructure. The user should know its behaviors, its compartments, its strengths, and its limits because they participated in building it.

Why It Matters

When Execution Moves Outward, Human Cognition Can Move Upward

From Doing the Work

A professional normally spends scarce attention executing one analysis, one workflow, or one line of reasoning at a time.

To Designing the Work

When the digital cortex can execute a known method, the human can ask a higher-order question: is this the best method, and what other methods should exist?

To Parallel Cognition

The harness can run several lenses, jobs, or specialist perspectives in parallel. The human can compare them, synthesize them, and design a better next round.

To New Context

LLMs are trained largely on artifacts of how people have already worked. As execution becomes cheaper, humans can spend more time exploring higher-order combinations and problem formulations that were previously too costly to practice routinely.

Make Cognitive Failure Modes Inspectable

When a method becomes explicit in jobs, phases, behaviors, and traces, failure modes such as inconsistency, premature closure, and confirmation bias become easier to observe and redesign. The digital cortex is not assumed to be unbiased; externalization makes cognition more inspectable and systematically improvable.

The analogy I use is the calculator. Offloading arithmetic did not make mathematicians irrelevant; it let them spend cognition on higher-level mathematics. I think agentic AI can produce a similar shift for flexible professional cognition.

The Abstraction Problem

Architectural Literacy Without Implementation Burden

I do not want a lawyer, scientist, founder, consultant, or filmmaker to learn shell programming before they can own a sophisticated harness. I do want them to understand its anatomy.

A user should understand what a hook is sensing, why a behavior needs state, which action a script exposes, when a specialist subagent should be dispatched, and how one behavior can use another behavior's interface. They do not need to write the hook payload parser, manipulate the JSON file, or implement the shell function themselves.

Human Layer

Describe the Cognition

The domain expert explains how they think: what they notice, what they remember, what decisions they make, what should be repeated, and where they want to stay in control.

Shared Language

Map It Into Architecture

Human and agent reason together using concepts such as plugins, sensors, state, jobs, phases, specialists, interfaces, verification, and memory.

Agent Layer

Compile Intent Into a Harness

The CLI agent translates that architectural intent into the platform's actual hooks, scripts, tools, skills, files, tests, and configuration.

The user learns the anatomy, not the syntax. The vocabulary can also evolve: different people can rename concepts or invent abstractions that fit how they naturally think, while the implementation underneath remains machine-operable.

Behavioral Architecture

A Plugin Starts With One Behavioral Objective

When I work with a person or team, I like to build the harness behavior by behavior. Each plugin owns a narrow objective. The anatomy below is a way to reason about that objective before worrying about code.

Objective

What consistent behavior should this part of the cortex produce?

Sensors — Hooks

What events or actions must the behavior notice? A Stop hook, for example, can sense an attempt to end work.

Internal State

What must the behavior remember about the current situation? State can remain private and be accessed through the plugin's own interface.

Actions — Scripts

What functions or verbs must the behavior be able to perform when its sensors fire or another component calls it?

Specialists — Subagents

Does the behavior need delegated investigation, execution, auditing, or another focused cognitive role?

Interface / API

What should other plugins be allowed to query or change? Mature behaviors compose through explicit interfaces rather than reaching blindly into one another's state.

Tests & Guards

Which parts of the behavior must remain reliable enough to earn deterministic enforcement and regression protection?

A simple generic example is unfinished-work behavior: sense a Stop attempt, query the job system, and refuse the stop while active or pending jobs remain. A client can understand and modify that design without knowing how the underlying hook is written.

As plugins accumulate, the harness becomes a library of learned behaviors. The user knows what their cortex already knows how to do, so new behavior can reuse existing state, functions, specialists, and interfaces instead of starting from scratch.

Work Architecture

Jobs Compartmentalize Work. Phases Compartmentalize Cognition.

Jobs

A meaningful piece of work becomes a durable compartment with an objective, interaction history, state, dependencies, completion logic, and long-horizon context. Closing a chat does not have to erase the work.

Phases

A job does not need to be one undifferentiated stream of tool calls. Different cognitive modes can have different goals, allowed actions, write authority, evidence, and transition rules.

Cycles

Some work closes in one cognitive cycle. Other work needs multiple cycles, with persistent plans and learning carried from one pass into the next.

Custom Cognition

OPEVC — Observe, Plan, Execute, Verify, Condense — is one implementation. A user's work may justify new phases, different transitions, or a job-specific plugin with its own sensing and constraints.

The purpose is not to force everyone's cognition into my five labels. It is to give people a workable vocabulary for taking cognition they already perform in their profession and making it explicit enough that a CLI agent can reproduce, scale, and improve it.

Worked Example

From Doing One Patent Analysis to Designing Patent Analysis

1. Externalize the Existing Method

The professional explains how they currently analyze patents: what they inspect, how they compare claims, what risks matter, and where judgment enters.

2. Encode the Cognition

The method becomes jobs, phases, specialist perspectives, memory, and behavioral controls inside the harness.

3. Execute at Scale

The digital cortex can repeat that analysis, maintain multiple active jobs, or run several analytical lenses in parallel instead of forcing the human to choose one.

4. Human Moves Up a Level

The professional spends attention asking which analytical methods should exist, why they disagree, what combination is stronger, and what new lens should be tested next.

5. Feed the Learning Back

Useful lessons update the digital cortex: a plan matures, a new specialist appears, a repeated judgment becomes a plugin, or a soft behavior earns a harder guard.

The same pattern can apply to market research, techno-economic analysis, drug discovery, client onboarding, filmmaking, legal work, or any profession where the person already carries a valuable way of thinking.

Human in the Loop

Autonomy Is a Division of Cognitive Labor, Not the Goal

I often think about the operating relationship as roughly 80/20. The harness should handle the bulk of routine execution, state management, delegation, and known decisions. The person should be pulled back in where their judgment, preference, authority, or genuinely new cognition matters.

The ratio is not a law. The principle is that the digital cortex should reduce low-value cognitive repetition while preserving deliberate places for human feedback. Over time, what repeatedly no longer needs feedback can mature into the harness; what remains ambiguous stays with the person.

Engineering Principles

Patterns I Keep Testing Across Harnesses

Single-Concern Behavioral Units

Give a behavior a clear objective and boundary so it can be understood, tested, composed, and changed without making the entire harness opaque.

Soft Before Hard

Coach uncertain behavior first. Measure where it fails. Harden into deterministic enforcement only where the evidence justifies the friction and maintenance cost.

Persistent State Over Chat Memory

Important cognition should survive the conversation window in explicit files, job state, plans, summaries, and knowledge structures.

Consolidation as Learning

Working context should not grow forever. Experience needs a metabolism: preserve, synthesize, promote durable knowledge, update behavior, and discard what no longer deserves attention.

Human Authority Around the Substrate

The agent can participate in changing its own harness, but changes to the machinery that controls future behavior should remain inspectable, testable, permissioned, and recoverable.

Local Ownership

The model can be rented intelligence. The accumulated harness — the user's memory, behaviors, workflows, and institutional context — should be portable enough to remain the user's asset.

The problem I want to keep working on is the layer between raw agent primitives and human cognition: a stable, composable language that models can reliably operate and ordinary professionals can meaningfully reason about.

The Architectural Fork

The Web, Social Media, and the Choice AI Is Making Now

I think about AI's customization layer through two earlier digital trajectories. The analogy is not that the technologies are identical; the important question is where the durable personalization layer lives, who can inspect it, and who can leave with it.

Open Web

User-Side Software Became a Powerful Layer

Open standards and browsers — including open-source browsers — left much of the interaction layer on the user's device. Settings, extensions, local browsing state, and client behavior could be inspected, modified, replaced, or moved more independently from any one online service. That helped make the web a substrate many different parties could build on.

Social Media

Personalization Moved Into the Platform

Social platforms followed a different trajectory. Much of identity, social graph, behavioral history, recommendation, and personalization accumulated inside proprietary services. Users gained powerful experiences, but the customization layer became less portable, less inspectable, and more dependent on the company operating the platform.

AI

We Are Choosing the Architecture Now

AI can move in either direction. Memory, preferences, workflows, learned behaviors, permissions, and cognitive structure can accumulate mainly inside a provider — or the harness can remain a local or portable asset while different commercial or open-weight models act as interchangeable engines that animate it.

My preference is explicit: I want AI to follow the more user-owned trajectory. The model can be rented intelligence, an API, or an open weight. The durable customization layer — the digital cortex built from a person's memory, behaviors, workflows, judgments, and accumulated experience — should remain something the person can understand, evolve, and carry forward.

If that path becomes practical, harness cultivation becomes a common professional meta-skill. A filmmaker remains a filmmaker, a scientist remains a scientist, and a lawyer remains a lawyer. But each can increasingly spend part of their work designing methods, adding behaviors, shaping jobs and cognitive modes, reviewing failures, and deciding where their own judgment remains essential while the digital cortex executes more of the established process.

This is not a prediction that the future must look this way. It is a consequence I am trying to make possible. Internet literacy, spreadsheet literacy, and smartphone literacy became ordinary capabilities once their interfaces became learnable enough. My long-term bet is that harness literacy can become similar if we discover the right abstraction language — and if models become reliable enough at translating that language into working, inspectable infrastructure.

That is why I care about the abstractions now. The design choices being made around agents today influence whether future users merely operate somebody else's intelligence system or participate in constructing the cognitive infrastructure that extends their own agency.

Private Systems & Demonstrations

The Code Is Not All Public. The Systems Are Demonstrable.

Several of my more developed harnesses contain accumulated working context, team-specific architecture, or experimental substrate that I do not publish directly. In a technical discussion I can demonstrate working systems, architecture boundaries, execution traces, tests, failure/recovery behavior, and how the same conceptual idea changes across implementations.

I am especially interested in where these abstractions fail. The purpose of building multiple prototypes is to identify which concepts continue to make sense after the machinery underneath them changes.

My default way of working is experimental: define the behavior, build a mechanism, observe failure, compare implementations, and keep the abstractions that survive.

I founded Hadosh Academy because education is also an architectural test.
