Family Games

A private illustrated world for a real family—not an open social game. Roughly 7–15 invited family members return to the same place over years, talk as characters, discover animals and hidden objects, create things together, play at different times, and leave family history inside the world itself.

Why make this?

A family call ends. A family world can remain.

The core idea is not “a zoo game with family members.” It is a persistent place that can replace some of the social function of a group call while giving the family something shared to return to afterward.

Every invited family member has a character in the same private world. When several people are online, they can walk together, split into smaller conversations, play, discover things, or work on a shared project. When only one person is online, the world still has useful activity—especially for children.

Nothing important resets when the session ends. Animals keep their names. Objects stay where they were created. Buildings remain. Memories can stay attached to places. The world becomes more specific to that family over time.

Starting inspiration

The simple loop was: walk, find an animal, name it.

The gameplay clip below helped inspire the visual direction and the basic pleasure of moving through an illustrated top-down environment and discovering animals. Family Games deliberately extends that loop much further.

Reference: We Build a Zoo gameplay clip

What Family Games keeps

  • Simple top-down movement through a friendly illustrated world.
  • The satisfaction of encountering animals in the environment.
  • Naming animals so discoveries become personally meaningful.
  • A low-friction visual style that works for children and adults.

What Family Games adds

  • Many family characters in the world at the same time, not one player.
  • Spatial voice and family communication.
  • A persistent shared world rather than a single-player progression loop.
  • Hidden illustrated objects that can be awakened into interactive/persistent objects.
  • Family authorship, asynchronous play, memory, permissions, and self-hosted ownership.
The world grows through use

Explore first. Then the family begins authoring the place.

The game can begin visually simple. Depth is introduced gradually through discovery and family-created objects rather than requiring a fully simulated 3D world from the beginning.

01

Explore

Move through illustrated regions and notice animals, landmarks, paths, and hidden hotspots.

02

Discover

Some flat background elements are latent objects. Finding one can make it interactive and attach a discoverer tag.

03

Name & personalize

Animals can be named by family vote. Family members can attach a recorded sound or short voice to them.

04

Gain authoring powers

A wand or similar mechanic can let members reveal, place, or create persistent objects. Child access can remain permissioned.

05

Build together

Resources and family decisions can create shared structures—homes, bridges, gardens, landmarks, play areas, or other persistent spaces.

06

Let time matter

Animals, structures, abilities, traditions, and places can change over months and years, giving the world visible history.

Why this is a family system

Live conversation, asynchronous play, and household authority coexist.

The project needs to work for adults and children without becoming either a generic video call or a children's game that adults have no reason to enter.

When the family is together

  • Spatial voice: nearby characters hear one another; conversations can split naturally by location.
  • Regroup: a family-wide call/shout can bring everyone back together.
  • Cooperative activities: exploration, voting, construction, games, celebrations, and shared decisions.
  • Visible presence: who is nearby and who can hear should be legible, not hidden.

Between family gatherings

  • Children: educational puzzles, exploration, creative tasks, animal care, or games.
  • Adults: leave objects, messages, small challenges, or contributions for later.
  • World continuity: solo activity can create surprises or progress that the whole family sees next time.
  • Agent-generated activities: future games can use the family's actual world and history, with adult review before persistent changes.
Family permissions matter. Adult and child accounts can have different authoring and communication powers. The household—not the software company—should define those rules.
A world that becomes an archive

Family memory stays connected to places and objects.

The long-term value is not only game progress. It is that ordinary play slowly creates a navigable history of the family.

Provenance

Objects can remember who discovered, named, built, or authored them.

Place-based memories

Recordings, sounds, notes, or events can remain attached to the place where they happened.

Traditions

Birthdays, recurring gatherings, family rituals, and shared projects can leave visible traces instead of resetting each year.

Changing world

Animals can age or gain abilities; structures can evolve; old objects can become markers of earlier family periods.

Session highlights

The harness could prepare short family-session summaries or videos, but private family media should remain reviewable before it is preserved or shared.

Generational continuity

If the world lasts long enough, younger family members can encounter objects and stories created before they were old enough to remember them.

The family owns the world

Private family history should not depend on one company's account database.

The intended architecture is a portable private web app/PWA that a family can host on a VPS, home server, or NAS—or use through a managed setup that remains exportable.

World

Map, objects, progression, buildings, animals, permissions, and family rules.

Media

Voice clips, recordings, images, generated highlights, and other private family assets.

Memory

Any harness/agent memory learned from the family and its history.

Extensions

Custom games, authored content, household rules, and future family-created mechanics.

Product packaging is still open: direct self-hosting, assisted VPS deployment, or optional managed hosting can all be explored. The non-negotiable direction is portability and family ownership.

Still in design

The unresolved questions are part of the project.

The concept is intentionally not pretending to be a finished game specification.

Return ritual

What makes a family want to reopen the world every week or month rather than use it once?

Adult / child balance

Which activities are interesting to both, and which should be intentionally age-specific?

Economy

How much earned value stays personal versus entering a family chest? Should that split vary by resource or project?

Privacy

What should the world remember automatically, what requires consent, and what should never become persistent memory?

AI authority

Which new games or objects can the harness generate on its own, and which persistent changes require an adult or family vote?

Hosting

How technical can self-hosting be before the product stops being useful to ordinary families?
