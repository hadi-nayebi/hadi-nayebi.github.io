The experiment

The harness produces. People give it judgment.

Research, data processing, maps, scripts, narration, and draft assembly can be highly automated. Publication cannot. Hadi performs that editorial role during launch. The community loop below is the next operating layer being designed; it activates after 500 project subscribers and enough useful feedback.

  1. 01

    The harness prepares a draft

    A participant may receive a script, chart, map, audio segment, or rough cut by email.

  2. 02

    A human reviews one bounded question

    Is the claim supported? Is the pacing clear? Which visual feels like slop? What context is missing?

  3. 03

    The decision becomes traceable input

    The harness records what changed and prepares the next revision. Reviewers can also assess other reviews.

  4. 04

    A human still controls release

    No public upload, economic activation, or governance change happens merely because an agent proposed it.

A real contribution

Turn a moving chart into something a person remembers.

A chart around 1994 may change meaning when it marks the arrival of widespread public internet access. A reviewer can propose that historical annotation, attach a credible source, and explain why it improves the story.

That is the work: not clicking “like,” but adding context, checking evidence, and shaping taste before publication.

Operating now

Production and public design

  • A production harness and rendered video backlog exist.
  • Hadi edits and approves the introduction and first remade test videos.
  • The launch does not wait for project or YouTube subscribers.
  • People can critique the model and request participation.
  • Early feedback improves the harness and future editorial workflow.
  • The preferred low-cost operating shape is one private VPS, with email as the primary project interface and no hosted database unless the workflow proves it needs one.
Proposed

Points and shared value

  • Points would recognize verified editorial contributions.
  • A project reserve would cover production and emergencies first.
  • A future surplus-sharing model may allocate up to 90% after costs and reserves.
  • Manager work is currently modeled at $42/hour with a future published monthly cap.
  • Individual and manager caps, war-chest overflow, and the $42 rate are design proposals, not current entitlements.
Open for discussion

Legal and governance design

  • Worker, contractor, contest, tax, securities, privacy, and state-law classification.
  • Eligibility, identity verification, payout methods, and dispute handling.
  • How contribution quality is scored without rewarding platform engagement.
  • What milestones must be met before any economic program activates.

No revenue-sharing or gift-card program is active. Nothing on this page is an offer, employment agreement, contest, security, or promise of payment. Any future economic stage requires written rules, appropriate legal and tax review, participant consent, and an announced effective date.

Revenue scenarios: a planning model, not a forecast

These examples use assumed RPM bands to make the allocation design testable. They are not promises, channel averages, or a prediction of Crime Cartography revenue. Actual RPM depends on audience, geography, advertiser suitability, monetized views, seasonality, and other revenue sources.

Pilot

20 videos × 10k monthly views = 200k views.

Assumed RPM$1.50–$4
Gross model$300–$800

Traction

20 videos × 50k monthly views = 1m views.

Assumed RPM$2.50–$6
Gross model$2,500–$6,000

Strong month

20 videos × 250k monthly views = 5m views.

Assumed RPM$3–$8
Gross model$15,000–$40,000

Planning formula: views ÷ 1,000 × assumed RPM = modeled platform revenue. Costs, manager hours and cap, taxes/accounting, and the war-chest minimum come first. Only the remaining distributable surplus could enter a future allocation rule. Read the full scenario model · Comment on assumptions

YouTube’s official documentation defines RPM as revenue per 1,000 views after revenue share and warns that earnings are not guaranteed. Long-form Watch Page revenue share is 55% of net ad revenue for eligible partners; ad revenue eligibility requires 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours or 10 million Shorts views. The project’s 100/500 seat choices are internal governance decisions, not YouTube monetization thresholds.
