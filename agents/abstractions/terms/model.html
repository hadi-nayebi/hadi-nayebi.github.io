Model

Current definition

In Hadosh Academy, a model is the parametric information-processing compartment of an agentic system and the category to which large language models belong. Its learned capabilities, representations, tendencies, knowledge, memory, and any reasoning performed in parameter or latent space are encoded in or produced through its parameters. A model receives supplied context, performs inference, and returns model-originated output to that shared interface. The output may be language, media, a prediction, classification, score, probability, representation, structured decision, action proposal, reasoning trace, or another inferred result. A model may be commercial, open-weight, provider-hosted, private, local, general, specialized, or fine-tuned to operate a harness. Architecture, training data and method, size, context capacity, modality, provider, and deployment affect a particular model’s capabilities without defining the compartment. Hadosh Academy treats model capability as a replaceable utility: powerful and necessary, but not the complete agentic system. Its utility becomes effective through interaction with a harness. A model may contain parametric memory and may generate explicit reasoning. When reasoning remains in latent or parameter space, it remains model-side. When it is emitted as text or another inspectable representation, it enters context and can be surrounded by harness mechanisms. A model output can propose or describe an action, but it is not by itself proof of truth, authorization, execution, verification, or completion. The boundary is intentionally practical rather than a claim that every system must distribute cognition identically. It lets people locate parametric behavior, evaluate it independently, replace it, and avoid assigning it responsibilities that another compartment can perform more reliably.
