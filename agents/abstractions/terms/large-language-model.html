Large language model (LLM)

Current definition

A large language model, or LLM, is a model trained to predict or generate language tokens from context. Additional training may help it follow instructions, select tools, produce structured output, or perform reasoning-like operations. In a harness, the LLM can interpret context and propose what should happen next, but fluent output does not prove factual correctness, permission, execution, or completion. The LLM is therefore treated as a replaceable, probabilistic capability—not as the agent, its memory, or its authority. The harness supplies durable context, state, tools, permissions, and verification around it. Future models may use different outputs or internalize more procedure, but practical agency still requires a governed connection between learned capability and action.
