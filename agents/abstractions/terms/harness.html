Harness

Current definition

In Hadosh Academy, a harness is the non-parametric information-processing compartment around model capability. It is composed from explicit, inspectable structures such as text, files, commands, code, rules, hooks, jobs, state, tools, permissions, workflows, interfaces, tests, histories, and recovery mechanisms. It constructs and supplies context to models, receives model-originated output, and determines what is interpreted, stored, removed, routed, executed, verified, rejected, or returned in later context. The harness turns model inference into continuing, bounded, and effective work. It can preserve durable memory, identity, responsibilities, authority, evidence, corrections, methods, and accumulated experience while models are replaced or assigned to different jobs, phases, or subtasks. A tool-call output is model inference; the software that authorizes and performs the call is harness behavior. A conditional hook that injects information is harness memory combined with a trigger. Harnesses may be thin or extensive, local or distributed, provider-controlled or user-controlled, textual or heavily coded, and they may surround different stages of cognition, including exposed reasoning traces. Hadosh Academy does not prescribe one universal configuration. It favors arrangements that move durable customization, inspectability, intervention, recovery, and practical control toward the user’s own harness. A harness does not replace parametric inference, and more structure is not automatically better. Its components are separated so their logic, state, authority, failures, and effects can be evaluated and improved independently. The filesystem is a current practical body for this durable structure, not the only possible implementation. The defining role is the non-parametric organization that carries the user’s relationship, continuity, and control around replaceable model capability.
