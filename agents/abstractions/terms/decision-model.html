Decision model

Current definition

A decision model is a model exposed through a bounded interface that evaluates supplied state against caller-defined questions and returns typed judgments with declared uncertainty for use inside explicit software logic. A judgment may estimate whether a condition holds, choose among defined options, rank candidates, score an ordered rubric, or use another bounded form whose meaning is declared by the caller. The term describes the model’s interface and role in a harness, not one internal architecture: a specialized model, large language model, ensemble, or future learned system may realize the interface. Typed output constrains shape, not truth. The harness selects and minimizes the state, defines the questions and criteria, validates the returned schema, interprets probabilities or scores through explicit policy, handles ambiguity, abstention, timeout, and failure, and retains authority over side effects. A decision model can supply one inference step inside a hook or workflow, but it does not by itself verify a claim, authorize an action, execute a transition, or own source-of-truth state.
