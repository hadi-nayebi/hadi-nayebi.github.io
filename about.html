Why an Academy

AI moves fast. The tools and models change every month. But the professionals who need them most — lawyers, consultants, researchers, founders, project managers, filmmakers — do not have time to become full-time AI engineers.

Hadosh Academy exists to close a different gap: not “teach everyone to code,” but make the architecture behind durable agent systems understandable enough that users can describe, inspect, and evolve their own harnesses with a CLI agent doing more of the lower-level implementation.

The user learns the anatomy, not the syntax.

From Chatbot to Digital Cortex

Most people still use AI as a chatbot — a helpful stranger they visit when they have a question. A CLI agent adds tools and access to a local environment. A harness adds the durable structure around that runtime: jobs, memory, behaviors, authority, verification, context, and learned ways of working.

The long-term goal is a digital cortex whose durable personalization layer belongs to the user. Models can change. CLI frameworks can change. The accumulated harness should remain understandable, inspectable, and portable enough to stay the user's asset.

The LLM can be rented intelligence. The harness is what grows around your life and work.

Your Brain, Extended

Two Ways to Start

Clone a mature public Seed

A future stable Seed can be a real product: clone the base, learn its existing architecture, then customize its cognition and jobs. This path is efficient for people comfortable inheriting a mature vocabulary and behavior stack.

Build a harness from scratch

For direct individual or team work, I often prefer a thinner starting point. Earlier systems become pattern libraries while the new user's own terminology, behaviors, plugin anatomy, memory, jobs, and authority are built with them involved from the beginning.

Both can produce user ownership. The difference is whether the architecture is inherited first and learned afterward, or learned while it is being formed.

For Every Professional

Seed agents are for people with valuable domain knowledge, not just programmers. The important question is how to externalize your way of working into a structure you understand well enough to keep improving.

A tax attorney and a documentary filmmaker do not need to end up with the same architecture. One may clone a mature Seed and specialize it. Another may cultivate a different harness from scratch with terminology and compartments that fit their own work. The stable layer is the useful abstraction, not compulsory sameness.
